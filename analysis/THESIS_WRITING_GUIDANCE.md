## Thesis Writing Guidance for AI CLI Tool

To ensure the AI tool generates high-quality, supported text, emphasize the following principles, drawing from the source materials:

1.  **Metric Rigor and Context:** When reporting results, always justify the metric choice. Use **Intersection over Union ($\text{IoU}$)** and $\text{F1}$ Score to assess geometric prediction quality, as $\text{IoU}$ is a proven metric for plan segmentation/overlap evaluation. Use scatter plots and confusion matrices to detail performance across different data bins, as demonstrated in the $\text{ANN}$ results.
2.  **Architectural Transparency:** Every component ($\text{DINO}$ Encoder, $\text{Feature Fusion}$, $\text{Temporal Transformer}$, $\text{Decoder}$) must be defined with detail, including layer counts, activation functions ($\text{ReLU}$ is common), regularization ($\text{BN}$, $\text{Dropout}$), and parameter counts, similar to the documentation provided for Sequential, $\text{CNN}$-$\text{A}$, and $\text{UNET}$-$\text{XY}$ models.
3.  **Data Precedence ($\text{RENE}$):** When discussing the $\text{Landscape}$ and $\text{Weather}$ features, model the discussion on the meticulous detail provided for the $\text{RENE}$ 30-feature vector, ensuring every element is quantified (geometric properties, $\text{topological}$ properties, and $\text{project data}$).
4.  **Process Justification:** Clearly explain why techniques like the multi-phase training (Phase 1: Frozen $\to$ Phase 2: Fine-tune) are necessary—to efficiently leverage pretraining and adapt the model to fire-specific visual patterns while managing memory risk.
5.  **Interpretability Depth:** Ensure the interpretation moves beyond mere summary. The analysis must explicitly connect the model's internal mechanisms (attention weights) to the physical phenomena ($\text{RoE}$, wind, slope), thereby providing **clear and interpretable explanations of the factors that contribute to the prediction of extreme wildfire events**.
